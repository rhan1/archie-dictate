## v1.17 — 2026-10-19
- A headset audio-conversion fault can no longer crash the app. Building the converter, making the audio formats, allocating the audio buffers and the conversion itself now run inside Dictate's existing Objective-C exception guard, on both headset backends (engine and AUHAL), at startup and when the headset changes sample rate. On v1.16 a native exception in any of these steps aborted the process. Now that buffer is dropped, the log names the failed step (`headset_native_error backend=… stage=… exception=…`, never audio or text), and the same recorder converts the next buffer (`headset_native_recovered=1`). A converter that failed mid-conversion is rebuilt instead of reused. A failure during a sample-rate change is retried on the next pass. Tested offline in a separate child process for every step × backend × startup/change: RED on v1.16 (`child_signal=SIGABRT`), GREEN on 1.17 (`alive=1 second_buffers=1`). Normal microphone behaviour is unchanged.
- The test rig's speaker (`DictateBench rig-play`) reports `engine_error` instead of crashing if its private audio engine cannot be constructed.

## v1.18 — 2026-10-19
